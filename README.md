# Lumen Warden — Vertical Slice

A top-down 2D arena shooter prototype built in Unity (C#), mouse-aim + WASD, desktop.

## 1. Concept & Design

**Setting:** You are WARD-7, a small maintenance drone aboard the derelict botanical
ship *Verdance*. The crew evacuated after a bio-viral outbreak corrupted the ship's
own garden-tending drones into hostile "spore" creatures. The last living Seed-Core
still glows at the heart of the ship — and its light is exactly what's drawing the
infected drones in. You're the only thing left defending it.

This is a thin wrapper on purpose: two lines of flavor text per wave (shown in the
wave-start data) are enough to give the arena a reason to exist without costing any
extra production time.

**Core combat loop:** mouse-aim, WASD move, hold left-click to fire. The weapon has
no ammo count — instead it builds **heat** per shot and **overheats** if fired
continuously, forcing a tap-fire rhythm instead of just holding the button down.
Shift is a short dash on a cooldown, giving the player an "I need this now" escape
tool without adding a second weapon. That's the entire skill expression: aim,
manage heat, use the dash to reposition — simple to learn, has a real skill
ceiling in how tightly you can weave the three together.

**Enemies (2 types, deliberately just 2 for the slice):**
- **Chaser** (magenta diamond) — beelines for the player, deals contact damage.
  Forces movement decisions.
- **Shooter** (orange ring) — holds a preferred range and fires projectiles at the
  player. Forces the player to break line of sight or close the distance.
  Mixing the two is what creates depth: pure Chasers is a kiting puzzle, adding
  Shooters means standing still to fight them is dangerous too.

**Loop structure — wave-based survival, one arena:** chosen over a level-based
clear or a roguelite run because it needs the least content to feel complete. One
scene, one spawn manager, and a `WaveData` list is the entire content pipeline —
no level-select flow, no upgrade-balancing pass. It still ramps difficulty cleanly
through enemy count/mix and spawn cadence.

**Win / lose:**
- **Lose:** player HP reaches 0 → "The Seed-Core Has Fallen" screen.
- **Win:** all 6 waves cleared → "Garden Secured" screen.
- Both show final score and a Restart button.

**Progression across 6 waves** (`Assets/Data/Waves/*.asset`, built by the scene
builder tool below):

| Wave | Composition | Spawn interval | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 Chasers | 1.0s | Introduces contact damage |
| 2 | 5 Chasers | 0.8s | Tests kiting under pressure |
| 3 | 4 Chasers + 2 Shooters | 0.7s | Introduces ranged threat |
| 4 | 6 Chasers + 3 Shooters | 0.6s | Mix pressure ramps |
| 5 | 8 Chasers + 4 Shooters | 0.5s | Heat management becomes critical |
| 6 | 10 Chasers + 6 Shooters | 0.4s | Final bloom |

No upgrade/meta-progression system in the slice — see Scope Guardrails.

## 2. Art Direction

**Style:** flat vector / minimal. Every sprite is a solid-color primitive shape
(circle, diamond, ring, triangle, square) generated **at runtime** by
`Assets/Scripts/Utils/ShapeSpriteFactory.cs` — no external art files needed at all,
so there's nothing to draw before the mechanics can be tested.

**Palette:**
- Background / floor: near-black navy (`#0D0E17` camera, `#171929` floor tile)
- Player: soft cyan (`#80D9FF`) with a bright white-cyan muzzle marker
- Chaser enemy: magenta-pink (`#D94D80`)
- Shooter enemy: amber (`#FFB333`)
- Player projectile: cyan (`#73F2FF`)
- Enemy projectile: rose (`#FF668C`)
- Impact VFX: warm yellow-white; death VFX: magenta burst
- Walls: cool slate (`#333952`)

Mood: cold, quiet, slightly clinical ship interior, punctuated by warm/bright
accent colors on anything that matters (player, hits, damage).

**Minimum sprite set** (all procedural, listed here as if they were art assets):
player ship, 2 enemy shapes, 2 projectile shapes, 1 wall/floor tile, 2 particle
burst styles (impact, death). That's the entire visual footprint of the slice.

## 3. Technical Implementation

### Project structure

```
Assets/
  Scripts/
    Core/     Health, IDamageable, GameManager
    Combat/   Projectile, Wall, HitFlash, DamageFeedback
    Player/   PlayerController, WeaponController, PlayerHealth
    Enemies/  EnemyAI (Chaser/Shooter behavior)
    Waves/    WaveData (ScriptableObject), WaveManager
    UI/       HUDController, GameOverUI, FillBar, ScreenShake
    Utils/    ShapeSpriteFactory (runtime flat-vector sprite generation)
  Editor/
    SceneBuilder.cs   -- see "Building the scene" below
  Data/Waves/          -- WaveData assets (generated by SceneBuilder)
  Prefabs/             -- generated by SceneBuilder
  Scenes/              -- generated by SceneBuilder
```

Naming: PascalCase for scripts/types, one MonoBehaviour per file, namespaces
mirror folders (`Game.Core`, `Game.Combat`, `Game.Player`, `Game.Enemies`,
`Game.Waves`, `Game.UI`, `Game.Utils`).

### Core systems

- **`Health`** — shared HP/damage component (`IDamageable`) used by both the
  player and enemies, with `OnHealthChanged` / `OnDamaged` / `OnDied` events.
- **`GameManager`** — singleton game-state machine (Playing/Won/Lost) + score.
- **`PlayerController`** — WASD movement via `Rigidbody2D.velocity`, aims an
  `AimPivot` child at the mouse cursor every frame, Shift-dash with cooldown.
- **`WeaponController`** — fire-rate cooldown + heat/overheat state machine,
  spawns `Projectile` instances.
- **`EnemyAI`** — single script, `EnemyBehavior` enum (Chaser/Shooter) picks
  chase-and-melee vs. hold-range-and-shoot behavior.
- **`WaveManager`** — coroutine-driven: waits, spawns a wave's enemies at
  spawn-point transforms, waits until all are dead, advances; calls
  `GameManager.ReportAllWavesCleared()` after the last wave.
- **`Projectile`** — trigger-collider mover; faction check (`Player`/`Enemy`)
  decides valid targets so enemy fire doesn't hit other enemies.

### Level/encounter data

Encounters are authored as `WaveData` ScriptableObjects (`Assets/Data/Waves`) —
enemy composition, spawn interval, delay before the wave, and a flavor-text
string. Designers add waves by right-clicking →
`Create > Game > Wave Data`, no code changes required.

### UI

`HUDController` drives a health bar and heat bar (custom `FillBar` — a plain
Image pair driven by anchor stretch, avoiding the full Unity `Slider` prefab
hierarchy) plus score/wave text. `GameOverUI` shows the win/lose panel with
final score and a Restart button that reloads the scene.

### Juice

- `HitFlash` — brief white flash on any sprite that takes damage.
- `DamageFeedback` — wires a `Health`'s events to hit-flash, screen shake, and
  a particle burst on death.
- `ScreenShake` — lightweight camera-jitter coroutine, singleton on the
  main camera.
- Small particle bursts on projectile impact and enemy death (flat-colored,
  no textures, matching the art direction).

### Building the scene

There is no hand-authored `.unity` scene file in this repo — instead,
`Assets/Editor/SceneBuilder.cs` adds a menu command that assembles the entire
arena procedurally (camera, player, walls, spawn points, all prefabs, wave
manager, HUD) from code, using the runtime sprite factory for art. This keeps
the whole vertical slice reproducible from script alone instead of a large,
hard-to-review binary scene diff.

**To generate and play the slice:**
1. Open this folder as a project in Unity Hub (2022.3 LTS recommended).
2. In **Edit > Project Settings > Player > Active Input Handling**, set it to
   `Input Manager (Old)` or `Both` — the scripts use the legacy `Input` class.
3. In the menu bar: **Tools > Vertical Slice > Build Arena Scene**. This
   creates `Assets/Scenes/Arena.unity`, saves all prefabs/wave data, and adds
   the scene to Build Settings.
4. Open `Assets/Scenes/Arena.unity` if it isn't already open, and press Play.

Re-running the menu command regenerates everything from scratch (safe to do
after pulling script changes).

## 4. Scope Guardrails

**In scope for this vertical slice:**
- One fully playable arena, one weapon, two enemy types, 6 authored waves.
- Win/lose states, score, HUD, restart flow.
- Procedural flat-vector art and juice (hit flash, shake, particles).

**Cut / explicitly out of scope** (flagged so they don't creep in):
- **Multiple weapons or a loadout system** — the heat mechanic already gives
  the single weapon a skill curve; a second weapon means balancing two damage
  curves and adding UI for switching. Cut version: stays a single weapon.
- **Meta-progression / upgrades between waves** — this was the roguelite
  option in the original design choice and was explicitly not picked, for the
  same reason: it adds a whole upgrade-selection UI and balancing pass on top
  of the base loop. If added later, bolt it onto `WaveManager`'s
  between-wave `WaitForSeconds` as a pause point.
- **More than 2 enemy types** — a third type (e.g. a tanky bruiser or a
  suicide-rusher) is cheap to add later (`EnemyAI` already generalizes via the
  `EnemyBehavior` enum + `WaveData`), but each new type is another thing to
  balance against the other two, so it's deferred past the slice.
- **Level select / multiple arenas** — one arena is enough to prove the loop;
  a second arena is pure content, not new systems.
- **Pickups/health regen** — deliberately absent so wave difficulty is the
  only tuning lever; add health pickups only if playtesting shows waves 5-6
  are unfairly punishing.
